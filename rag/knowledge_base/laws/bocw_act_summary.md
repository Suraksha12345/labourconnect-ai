# Building and Other Construction Workers (BOCW) Act, 1996 & Karnataka Rules, 2017

Source: Chief Labour Commissioner (clc.gov.in) and Karnataka BOCW Rules 2017

## Purpose
The BOCW Act, 1996 is a central law that regulates employment conditions and provides social security for building and construction workers across India, who otherwise fall into the unorganised labour category. It also created the Cess Act, 1996, which levies a cess on construction costs to fund state Welfare Boards.

## Welfare Board Functions
Under Section 22 of the Act, State Welfare Boards are empowered to:
- Provide immediate assistance to a beneficiary in case of an accident
- Pay pensions to beneficiaries who have reached 60 years of age
- Sanction loans/advances for house construction, within prescribed limits
- Pay Group Insurance premiums on behalf of beneficiaries
- Fund children's education
- Cover major medical treatment expenses
- Pay maternity benefits to female beneficiaries
- Fund other welfare measures as prescribed by the state

## Hours of Work, Records & Welfare Measures (Sections 28-39)
The Act mandates specific working conditions at construction sites, including:
- Fixed limits on normal working hours per day, with double-rate overtime pay
- Maintenance of employment/wage registers and muster rolls
- Provision of drinking water at worksites
- Separate latrines and urinals for workers
- Crèche facilities where required
- First-aid facilities at every site
- Canteens at larger sites
- A designated Safety Committee and Safety Officer for bigger establishments

## Registration Requirements (Karnataka Rules, 2017)
- Employers must maintain an employment register (Form X) for all construction workers
- Workers must be registered as beneficiaries under Section 12 of the Act
- Registration fees apply per employer, with additional fees for amendments
- Employers are prohibited from employing certain categories of persons in specified hazardous building work

## Karnataka-Specific Notes
Karnataka's 2017 Rules operationalise the central Act at the state level, detailing the exact procedure for registration, safety compliance (fencing of machinery, dust/fumes control, illumination of passageways, lifting equipment certification), and the administrative structure of the state Welfare Board (headquartered in Bengaluru).
