# National Building Code of India (NBC) - Construction & Fire Safety

Source: Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), National Building Code of India 2016

## What It Is
The National Building Code (NBC) is India's primary reference standard for planning, constructing, and maintaining buildings safely. It's published by the Bureau of Indian Standards and is meant to be adopted into state/local building bye-laws — it is recommendatory at the national level, but becomes legally binding once a state or municipal body incorporates it into local regulations.

## Part 7 - Constructional Practices and Safety (most relevant to site workers)
This section covers safe practices specifically during the construction process itself — not the finished building, but the work of building it. It addresses:
- Safe site organization and construction planning to reduce risk to workers and site personnel
- Safe use of construction equipment and machinery
- Scaffolding, excavation, and temporary structure safety during the build phase
- General site safety management practices

This is the part most relevant to daily wage construction workers, since it deals with safety *during* the work itself, complementing the BOCW Act's worker welfare provisions.

## Part 4 - Fire and Life Safety (relevant once building is occupied/multi-storey)
This section deals with safety from fire in completed buildings — relevant for larger construction projects (multi-storey buildings, commercial complexes) that LabourConnect workers might be employed on. Key concepts:
- Buildings are classified by occupancy type (residential, industrial, assembly, etc.), which determines what fire safety measures apply
- Requirements for exit routes (exit access, exit, and exit discharge) so occupants can safely evacuate during fire, smoke, or panic situations
- Minimum requirements for firefighting installations (fire pumps, sprinkler systems, fire water storage) scaled to the building's size and occupancy type
- Fire-resistance requirements for staircases, fire doors, and enclosed escape routes in taller buildings
- Special provisions for higher-risk locations like commercial kitchens and underground parking

## Why This Matters for LabourConnect
- For **electricians, masons, and general construction labour**, Part 7's site safety practices are the most directly applicable — this is the basis for safe scaffolding, safe equipment handling, and organized site practices
- For workers on **larger or multi-storey projects**, awareness of Part 4's fire safety requirements (like clear exit routes and not blocking staircases with materials) helps them understand why a site may enforce certain rules
- This is a **general national standard**, separate from and in addition to the Karnataka-specific BOCW Rules, 2017 (which focus specifically on labour welfare and basic site amenities). NBC is broader engineering/construction practice; BOCW is labour-focused welfare and safety law.

## Practical Note
Because NBC provisions become binding only once adopted into local building bye-laws, actual enforcement can vary by city/municipality. Workers and contractors should also check their local municipal corporation's building bye-laws for the specific, legally enforceable safety requirements in their area, rather than relying on NBC alone.
